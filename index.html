How it Works

A Galileo thermometer contains a sealed glass cylinder filled with a clear liquid and several sealed glass spheres of slightly different densities. Each sphere is attached to a small metal tag engraved with a temperature.

As the temperature of the surrounding air changes, the density of the liquid changes more than the density of the spheres. When the liquid becomes denser (cooler), the spheres float higher. When the liquid becomes less dense (warmer), the spheres sink.

The temperature is read from the lowest floating sphere (or the average of the lowest floating and highest sinking sphere when they are close together).

A Bit of History

Named after Galileo Galilei, who discovered that the density of a liquid changes with temperature. The instrument as we know it was refined in the 17th century by the Accademia del Cimento in Florence. It remains one of the most beautiful ways to display temperature.

Try It

Drag the slider slowly. Watch the spheres rise and fall as the liquid density changes. The currently indicated temperature is shown above the slider and highlighted on the tags.
